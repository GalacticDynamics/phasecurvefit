Track uncertainty#

A fitted track comes from one draw of the data, and a different draw from the same stream would give a slightly different track. This page explains where that uncertainty comes from, why phasecurvefit estimates it with a bootstrap of the whole pipeline, and how well the resulting bands are calibrated. Put an uncertainty band on the track shows how to compute them.

Where the uncertainty enters#

The track passes through four stages, and each adds its own uncertainty.

  • The ordering. The MST and the SOM are discrete. A small change in the data can change which samples are linked, which are called background, and where the ordering starts and stops.

  • Membership. A sample counted as a member pulls the track towards it, and a sample counted as background doesn’t.

  • The encoder’s \(\gamma\). Where a sample lands along the track decides which part of the track it pulls on.

  • The decoder. Even with the ordering, membership and \(\gamma\) fixed, the track is fitted to noisy positions.

The first three are discrete choices or come from networks trained on them, so no likelihood connects them to the data. A posterior over the decoder’s weights alone holds them fixed.

Every method below the bootstrap puts a posterior on the decoder only, given one ordering, one membership and one set of \(\gamma\). A posterior also needs a likelihood. The default joint loss is a weighted sum of a squared distance and a velocity-alignment term, with no noise model, so a Gaussian noise level has to be added for any of them. The mixture model of Outlier rejection with a mixture model supplies one, \(\sigma(\gamma)\), but only when it is turned on. With per-sample weights that are not counts, such as image intensities, the weighted loss is a pseudo-likelihood and its curvature isn’t an uncertainty (Density fields).

The decoder’s last layer is linear, so with a Gaussian likelihood and prior the posterior over it is Gaussian, and the Laplace approximation is exact. HMC on the last layer would sample the same distribution, so it was not run separately. Full-network HMC, VI, MC dropout, SWAG and a GP on the residuals were not run. They all leave the ordering, membership and \(\gamma\) fixed, and the measurements below show that the decoder alone carries only part of the variance.

Measured calibration#

The test data are a sine-shaped arc, \(x = 10 s\), \(y = 2 \sin(1.5 \pi s)\), with 400 samples, a Gaussian width of 0.2 across the track, and velocities along it. There were 12 independent datasets and 20 members per ensemble, with the default TrainingConfig. At 46 points along the true curve, I took each member’s signed offset along the true normal, and counted the true curve as covered when the offset 0 lay inside the central 68% or 95% of the members. The prototypes are in prototypes/track_uncertainty/ in the repository.

The bootstrap row is bootstrap_track() as it ships. The other two rows were measured on an earlier version of the pipeline, before fit_track() used a SOMMixture by default. On that version a bootstrap built from weights instead of rows covered 0.59 and 0.85, so the comparison between methods holds.

The error of a single fit was 0.038 rms. The tube about the reference fit that radius() returns covered the truth 0.58 and 0.88 of the time, with a median 68% radius of 0.032.

Training randomness alone gives about 30% of the bootstrap’s variance, and the last-layer posterior about the same. Most of the variance comes from the data and the stages that depend on it, so a method that fixes those stages gives bands that are too narrow.

What the bootstrap misses#

The bootstrap gets the variance right. Its spread at each point averaged 0.028, and the scatter of a single fit across the 12 datasets averaged 0.030. The coverage falls short because of bias. The fit’s mean offset from the true curve had an rms of 0.022, close to the spread, and it was largest where the track bends, 0.047 at \(s = 0.83\), and at the far tip, 0.074 at \(s = 0.95\). The 68% coverage dropped to 0.25–0.33 at those points. A track fitted to a resample is smoothed in the same way as the reference, so the bootstrap cannot see this bias. Treat the band as a lower limit on the uncertainty where the track curves sharply.

On the earlier pipeline, with 4% interlopers spread over the field, the bands were wider (68% half-width 0.048) and covered 0.77 and 0.97 of the time, because which interlopers are drawn and clipped changes from member to member. That run changed two things at once, so it doesn’t separate the two effects, and it has not been repeated with the SOMMixture default.

Why repeated samples are passed as rows#

A bootstrap resample can be written as integer weights. bootstrap_track() passes repeated samples as repeated rows instead, so weights keep their own meaning, such as the density at each pixel, and a resampled sample keeps its weight.
