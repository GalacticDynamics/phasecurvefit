Reject outliers with the mixture model#

This guide shows how to train the autoencoder with a stream-plus-background mixture model, and read a calibrated membership probability for each sample. Outlier rejection with a mixture model explains why the default membership cannot reject outliers and how the mixture model fixes it. The outlier-rejection tutorial runs it end to end on a mock stream with injected interlopers.

Turn it on#

Membership modelling is opt-in: with membership=None, the default, training is unchanged. Pass a MixtureMembershipConfig in the TrainingConfig. Here the data are 200 points on an arc with 10 interlopers scattered around it. The epoch counts are small to keep the example fast; use the defaults, or more, on real data.

import jax
import jax.numpy as jnp
import phasecurvefit as pcf

ang = jnp.linspace(0.0, jnp.pi, 200)
k_bg, k_model, k_train = jax.random.split(jax.random.key(0), 3)
bg = jax.random.uniform(k_bg, (2, 10), minval=-6.0, maxval=6.0)
pos = {
    "x": jnp.concatenate([5 * jnp.cos(ang), bg[0]]),
    "y": jnp.concatenate([5 * jnp.sin(ang), jnp.abs(bg[1])]),
}
vel = {
    "x": jnp.concatenate([-jnp.sin(ang), jnp.zeros(10)]),
    "y": jnp.concatenate([jnp.cos(ang), jnp.ones(10)]),
}

ordering = pcf.order(pos, vel)
model = pcf.nn.PathAutoencoder.make(
    pcf.nn.StandardScalerNormalizer(pos, vel),
    gamma_range=ordering.gamma_range,
    key=k_model,
)

membership = pcf.nn.MixtureMembershipConfig(
    sigma_init=0.1,  # your guess at the stream width, in normalised units
    sigma_ceiling=(0.5, 0.1),  # shrink the width ceiling during training
    warmup_frac=0.3,  # fit the track before rejecting anything
)
config = pcf.nn.TrainingConfig(
    n_epochs_encoder=100,
    n_epochs_decoder=50,
    n_epochs_both=200,
    membership=membership,
    show_pbar=False,
)
result, _, losses = pcf.nn.train_autoencoder(
    model, ordering, config=config, key=k_train
)

Read the membership#

Use posterior_membership() on the trained network, result.model, not the encoder’s raw probability. It takes the samples as one array of positions and velocities in the normalised coordinates the network was trained in:

qn, pn = result.model.normalizer.transform(pos, vel)
ws = jnp.concatenate([qn, pn], axis=1)

q = pcf.nn.posterior_membership(result.model, ws)  # posterior, in [0, 1]
members = q > 0.5
print(q.shape)

(210,)

The encoder’s probability is only the prior: what it believes from a sample’s coordinates before seeing how far the sample landed from the track. posterior_membership folds in that distance. Hogg, Bovy & Lang (2010) recommend keeping the posterior as a weight rather than thresholding it.

Keep the two schedules#

Both settings below close off a way the fit can fail. Keep them on.

  • sigma_ceiling=(start, stop) caps the stream width from above, with a ceiling that shrinks during training. Without it, outliers a few widths from the track are absorbed by widening the stream instead of being rejected.

  • warmup_frac fits the track first, with every sample held as a member, and only then lets the model reject samples. Without it, training can declare the whole dataset background and never recover.

With membership set, the encoder’s \(\gamma\) is also frozen during the joint phase, because an encoder that keeps training can make the same collapse happen. Leave freeze_encoder_final_training at its default (Outlier rejection with a mixture model explains why).

Reject outliers in an image#

For an image, order the pixels with their values as weights (order a density field), then train with the mixture and the two settings a wide curve needs (Fill gaps and fit a track with the autoencoder):

config = pcf.nn.TrainingConfig(
    freeze_encoder_final_training=True,
    batch_size=1000,
    membership=pcf.nn.MixtureMembershipConfig(),
    show_pbar=False,
)

Pass the weighted ordering to train_autoencoder as above, and it reads the weights from there. A weight sets how much a pixel counts in the fit, not whether it is a member, so the faint pixels at the edges of the curve stay members (Density fields derives why).

On the density-field tutorial’s image, with pixels of 0.1 and three bright blobs added, ordered with RidgeOrderer() | SOMOrderer(n_prototypes=30) and trained with these settings, thresholding the posterior at 0.5 kept every pixel on the curve. It rejected every pixel of the two blobs 2.0 and 3.2 from the curve. The third blob’s centre is 0.59 from the curve, inside its half-width of 0.63 there, and the blob stayed a member.
